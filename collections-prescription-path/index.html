Do not assume an old debt is prescribed

Prescription depends on the type of debt and the facts, and it may be affected by events such as payments, acknowledgements or legal proceedings. The age of a listing alone does not prove that a debt is prescribed. Do not ignore court papers or rely on a template as a final legal determination.

Build a clear account history

Gather the original agreement or account details if available, statements, payment records, letters from the original creditor and any collection notices. Note when you last paid, whether you acknowledged the debt, and whether you received legal documents. Keep a timeline and distinguish what you know from what still needs confirmation.

What the educational pathway covers

The kit is described as a prescription assessor, collection-validation pack, payment-history reconciliation and escalation pathway. These resources help you frame questions, request relevant account information and organise a written query where you believe the balance, ownership or credit-report information may be wrong.

When to get qualified help

If a summons, judgment, threatened legal action or a disputed prescription issue is involved, consider advice from a qualified attorney or an appropriate consumer-support service. Do not use a general template as a substitute for advice on your specific legal position.

Frequently asked questions

Does a debt become prescribed after exactly three years?

Not in every situation. The applicable period and whether it has been interrupted depend on the debt type and facts. Seek qualified advice if unsure.

Can a collection account be challenged?

You can ask the creditor, collector or bureau to investigate specific inaccuracies and provide relevant supporting documents. A challenge does not guarantee deletion.

Does the kit contact debt collectors for me?

No. It is educational material for self-directed use and does not contact creditors, collectors or bureaus on your behalf.
