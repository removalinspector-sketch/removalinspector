How Long Does Adverse Information Stay on a Credit Report?

South African credit-bureau retention periods depend on the category of information, the event and applicable legal rules—not one universal blacklisting expiry date.

There is no single expiry date for every item people call “blacklisting”. South African credit records can contain payment-profile information, adverse classifications, judgments, debt-rearrangement information and other categories. Each category may have a different retention rule, and some entries can be removed earlier when a legal condition is met.

Do not confuse these different records

  • Payment profile: factual information about how an account was paid.
  • Adverse classification: a category describing certain negative conduct or enforcement action.
  • Judgment: a court record, with separate rules for rescission and retention.
  • Debt-rearrangement information: information connected to debt review, for which the Act contains a clearance-certificate process.

Why the category matters

Regulation 17 of the National Credit Regulations sets maximum display periods for specified categories. For example, the regulation provides a five-year maximum for payment-profile information and a one-year maximum for certain adverse classifications of enforcement action, subject to the wording and other applicable provisions. Other categories have their own rules. Do not apply the one-year period to every negative item or assume it automatically deletes an account balance or court judgment.

What can change the timeline?

  • The date the relevant event occurred or was recorded.
  • The precise category and description shown on the report.
  • Whether the entry is accurate and supported.
  • Whether a settlement, clearance certificate, court rescission or other legally relevant event has occurred.
  • Whether the bureau has received and processed the required evidence.

What to do now

  1. Get the latest report and identify the exact entry and category.
  2. Record the event date and compare it with the relevant retention rule.
  3. Gather the documents that prove any later event, such as payment, settlement, clearance or rescission.
  4. Ask the bureau or credit provider to explain the category and applicable retention basis in writing.
  5. If the information is inaccurate, use the formal dispute process and keep the reference number.
Important: Paying an account does not automatically erase its historical payment profile. A debt-review clearance certificate is not the same thing as proof that a judgment has been rescinded. Check the legal route that matches the entry.
