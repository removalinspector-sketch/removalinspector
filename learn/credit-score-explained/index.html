Credit scores · Guide 07

What does your credit score mean?

A credit score is a model-based summary of information in a credit file. It is not a universal pass mark, a legal finding or a guarantee that a lender will approve an application.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · General information

Scores are model-dependent

Credit bureaus and lenders may use different scoring models, data, score ranges and risk criteria. This means scores from different services may not match. A lender may also consider affordability, income, existing obligations, the requested product and its own credit policy.

What can influence a score?

Depending on the model and available data, relevant factors may include payment behaviour, amounts owed, account history, credit applications and the mix of credit accounts. The precise formula is usually proprietary. Do not assume that one factor has the same weight across every bureau or lender.

What a score cannot tell you

  • It does not prove that every account on the report is accurate.
  • It does not establish whether a debt is legally enforceable.
  • It does not guarantee credit approval or a particular interest rate.
  • It does not mean paying for a “score boost” will change a lender's decision.

Practical steps to manage credit

  1. Check your reports for factual errors.
  2. Pay obligations by their due dates where you can.
  3. Contact providers early if you anticipate difficulty paying.
  4. Review affordability before taking on additional credit.
  5. Limit unnecessary applications and understand the terms before applying.

Do not chase a number blindly

Focus on accurate records, affordable borrowing and consistent repayment behaviour rather than a particular score target. If an entry is wrong, use the bureau's formal dispute process and provide evidence. If the entry is accurate, ask the provider about legitimate options for managing the account.

Warning: Be cautious of services promising a guaranteed score increase or a secret method to erase accurate information.
