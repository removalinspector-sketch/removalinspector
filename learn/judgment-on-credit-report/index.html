A judgment appears on your credit report: what should you check?

A judgment is a court-related record, not simply an ordinary unpaid-account marker. Do not assume it can be removed by paying a company or sending a generic bureau dispute.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · Legal information, not legal advice

Obtain the underlying court information

Record the court name, case number, parties, date and amount shown on the report. Obtain the actual court order or a copy from the relevant court where possible. Compare the spelling of names, identity details, case reference and order details with the bureau entry.

Check whether the entry is accurate

Ask whether the judgment belongs to you and whether the reported details match the order. If the judgment was rescinded, varied or otherwise affected by a later court order, obtain the official document. A payment receipt alone is not the same as a court order rescinding a judgment.

Separate the court process from the bureau process

A credit bureau can investigate whether the information it reports is accurate and supported. It cannot act as a court and independently set aside a judgment. If you need the judgment rescinded or corrected at court, obtain advice from a qualified legal practitioner or the appropriate legal-aid service about the procedure that applies.

If the bureau record is wrong

Challenge the specific factual error and attach the relevant court documents. If the information is accurate but you dispute the underlying judgment, the legal remedy may need to be pursued through the court process rather than treating it as a routine data correction.

Documents to gather

  • Current credit report showing the entry.
  • Court name, case number and copy of the order.
  • Any later rescission, variation or correction order.
  • Relevant service documents or correspondence, where applicable.
  • Settlement or payment proof, if relevant to the facts.
Do not rely on a guarantee: No private credit-repair service can promise that a valid court judgment will be rescinded. Court outcomes depend on the facts, legal grounds and applicable procedure.

Official sources

  • Department of Justice and Constitutional Development
  • Legal Aid South Africa
  • National Credit Regulator
  • National Credit Act 34 of 2005
