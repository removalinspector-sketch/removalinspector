Can anyone guarantee credit-record removal in South Africa?

If a service promises guaranteed blacklisting or credit-record removal, pause before paying. “Blacklisting” is a common term, but the entry on your report could be a payment profile, arrears status, judgment, debt-review status or another type of consumer credit information. Each has different facts and possible next steps.

The key point: No blanket promise can establish that your specific record is wrong or eligible for removal. Ask what evidence and process support the proposed action, and be wary of claims that every listing can be removed quickly or automatically.

What can actually be challenged?

You can challenge credit information that you believe is inaccurate. Examples might include an account that is not yours, a balance that does not match the supporting records, incorrect dates, a status that was not updated, or debt-review information that does not reflect your actual position. These are examples, not a guarantee that a bureau will amend the record: the facts and supporting evidence matter.

Section 72 of the National Credit Act provides a process for challenging the accuracy of information held by a credit bureau. The National Credit Regulator’s guidance explains the evidence-based dispute process and escalation steps. Start with the NCR’s official disputed-information guidelines and the relevant bureau’s official instructions.

What a paid service can—and cannot—do

  • It may help you understand documents: an educational service can explain common terms, help you organise evidence and provide a checklist or template for you to review.
  • It cannot make an inaccurate claim true: a payment does not turn accurate information into an error, and a template alone does not prove that a record must be changed.
  • It should be clear about its role: ask whether the provider is a law firm, registered debt counsellor, bureau, or educational service. Do not assume a provider has authority to issue official documents or change bureau data.
  • It should explain the fee: request a written description of what is included, any extra costs, and what happens if the dispute is not successful.

Red flags to check before you pay

  1. A promise that every listing will be removed, regardless of the evidence.
  2. Pressure to pay immediately or claims of a secret shortcut.
  3. Requests for your banking password, one-time PIN or unnecessary identity information.
  4. No clear explanation of the service, total price, refund terms or provider identity.
  5. Claims that the company can issue a court order or a Form 19 clearance certificate when it has no authority to do so.

A safer checklist before taking action

  1. Obtain your current credit report and identify the exact entry and bureau.
  2. Write down what you believe is wrong and why.
  3. Gather relevant documents, such as statements, payment confirmation, a clearance certificate or court order where applicable.
  4. Submit a specific dispute using the bureau’s official channel and save the reference number.
  5. Read the written response. If unresolved, use the official escalation route and check any applicable deadlines.

For example, TransUnion South Africa’s dispute page explains that only inaccurate information may be removed through its investigation process; accurate negative information can remain for the period allowed by the applicable rules. Frequently asked questions

Can a company guarantee that my blacklisting will be removed?

Be cautious of blanket guarantees. The outcome depends on the record, the evidence and the process that applies to your case.

Can accurate negative information be removed because I paid a fee?

A fee does not make accurate information inaccurate. Use a dispute process when you have a genuine basis to challenge the information, and check the applicable rules.

Can I dispute credit information myself?

Yes. You can use the relevant bureau’s official process, provide evidence and keep a record of the response.
