How to Dispute Incorrect Information on a Credit Report

A practical South African checklist for identifying inaccurate credit information, gathering evidence and following the section 72 dispute process.

If a credit report contains information that is wrong, you can challenge its accuracy. The National Credit Act (NCA) provides a process for a consumer to ask for disputed information to be investigated. The strongest dispute identifies one specific error and supports it with relevant evidence.

Step 1: Identify the exact error

Write down the bureau, account or entry, the field that is wrong and what the correct information should be. Examples may include an account that does not belong to you, a balance that does not match the provider’s records, a payment status that has not been updated, or a duplicated entry. “Please clear my name” is too vague to investigate properly.

Step 2: Collect supporting documents

  • Recent credit report showing the disputed entry.
  • Account statements or a settlement letter.
  • Proof of payment and relevant transaction dates.
  • Identity-theft or fraud reference documents, where relevant.
  • Correspondence from the credit provider or bureau.

Send only documents relevant to the issue and use the bureau’s secure official submission channel.

Step 3: Lodge the dispute with the bureau

Section 72(1)(c) of the NCA gives consumers the right to challenge the accuracy of information held by a credit bureau. Section 72(3) requires the relevant party to take reasonable steps to seek evidence and, within the prescribed period, provide credible evidence supporting the entry or remove the information if credible evidence cannot be found, subject to the Act.

Understand the 20-business-day period

Regulation 20(2) states that the steps in section 72(3) must be taken within 20 business days after the dispute is filed. This is not a promise that every credit-profile problem will be fully resolved in 20 days: the outcome depends on the evidence and the type of issue.

If credible evidence is supplied and you still dispute the information, section 72(4) provides a further route: you may apply to the National Credit Regulator within 20 business days after receiving that evidence for the disputed information to be investigated as a complaint under section 136. Check the NCR’s current complaint requirements and forms.

Step 4: Keep a complete record

Save the submission, date, reference number, attachments, response and any follow-up. Once the bureau says the entry has been corrected, obtain an updated report and check the relevant entry rather than assuming every bureau has updated in exactly the same way.

Do not dispute accurate information just to try your luck. A dispute is for a genuine accuracy problem. If the information is correct but a later event has not been reflected, ask the credit provider or bureau about an update process and supply proof of that event.
