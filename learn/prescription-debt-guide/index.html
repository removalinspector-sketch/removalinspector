Prescribed debt in South Africa: what should you check?

Prescription can limit the time in which certain debts may be enforced. Whether a specific debt has prescribed cannot safely be decided from its age alone.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · General educational information

What is prescription?

South African prescription law sets time limits for enforcing different kinds of debts. The Prescription Act 68 of 1969 contains the general framework, but the applicable period and rules depend on the nature of the debt and relevant legal events. Some debts are subject to different periods or specific statutory rules.

Why the last payment date is not enough

The date of the last payment is only one part of the timeline. The date the debt became due, whether and when the creditor demanded payment, whether liability was acknowledged, whether a payment was made and whether legal process was served may all matter. Acknowledgement of liability or part-payment may interrupt prescription in circumstances provided by law. Legal proceedings can also affect the calculation.

Collect the underlying documents

  • Original credit agreement and any variations.
  • Full account statement showing charges, payments and dates.
  • Copies of acknowledgements, payment arrangements or settlement proposals.
  • Letters of demand, summonses and proof of service, if any.
  • Judgment and later court documents, if a judgment was granted.
  • Collection correspondence identifying the creditor and account.

Do not make assumptions before seeking advice

If you are unsure whether a debt may have prescribed, understand the consequences before acknowledging liability, signing a new arrangement or making a payment. This is not advice to ignore a valid debt. It is a reason to obtain the complete record and independent legal advice before taking a step that may affect your position.

Prescription and credit reporting are distinct questions

Whether a debt is enforceable and whether a particular credit-report entry is accurate are related but separate issues. The National Credit Regulations contain provisions concerning prescribed debt information, while the National Credit Act provides a process for challenging inaccurate credit information. Identify the underlying debt question and the precise information shown on the report.

Questions for a qualified adviser

  1. What type of debt is this and which prescription period applies?
  2. When did the debt become due?
  3. Was prescription interrupted or delayed by acknowledgement, payment or legal process?
  4. Was a summons or other process served, and what happened next?
  5. Does the credit report accurately reflect the relevant facts and rules?
Important: Do not rely on a generic “three-year rule” without checking the debt type and full timeline. A judgment debt raises different issues from an ordinary contractual account. Obtain legal advice for a specific prescription assessment.
