Section 72: the right to access and challenge credit records

Section 72 of the National Credit Act 34 of 2005 sets out important rights to inspect credit records and challenge the accuracy of information held by a credit bureau or the National Credit Register.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · Legal information summary, not legal advice

Right to inspect your credit record

Section 72(1)(b) provides a right to inspect a person's credit bureau or National Credit Register file without charge once within any 12-month period. It also provides for access in specified circumstances, including after successfully challenging information to verify whether it has been corrected. Other inspections may be subject to the bureau's applicable fee.

Right to challenge accuracy

Section 72(1)(c) allows a person to challenge the accuracy of information concerning them that is proposed to be reported or is already held by a bureau or the National Credit Register. The consumer may require the relevant organisation to investigate the accuracy of the challenged information without charge.

What happens after a challenge?

Section 72(3) requires the credit provider, bureau or National Credit Register to take reasonable steps to seek evidence supporting the challenged information. Within the prescribed period, it must provide a copy of credible evidence to the person who filed the challenge or remove the information and all record of it if it cannot find credible evidence, subject to the Act's provisions.

Regulation 20(2) specifies 20 business days after filing for the steps in section 72(3). This is not a guarantee that an entry will be removed: information supported by credible evidence may remain.

What if evidence is supplied?

Section 72(4) provides that within 20 business days after receiving a copy of evidence under section 72(3)(a), the person who challenged information held by a credit provider or bureau may apply in the prescribed manner and form to the National Credit Regulator for an investigation as a complaint under section 136. This is a separate period from the initial investigation timeframe.

What the section does not mean

  • It does not require deletion of all negative information on request.
  • It does not mean a consumer automatically wins a dispute by disagreeing with an entry.
  • It does not guarantee a higher score or approval for credit.
  • It does not replace court procedures or the specific rules that may apply to judgments and debt review.

Use the right with specific evidence

Identify the exact entry, explain the factual problem, attach relevant supporting records and keep the submission reference. If evidence is supplied in response, examine whether it relates to the same person, account, amount and period. If the issue remains unresolved, consult the NCR's current complaint guidance and consider qualified advice.

Accuracy note: This is a plain-language explanation. The current Act, regulations and applicable amendments control. Read the full text before relying on a statutory right in a particular case.
