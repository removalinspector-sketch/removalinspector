Debt review status after paying your debts: what to check

Completing payments is important, but it may not be the only step required to update a debt-review status. The applicable process depends on the arrangement, court documents and the status confirmed by the registered debt counsellor.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · General educational information

Separate payment completion from legal status

A consumer may have paid instalments under a debt-rearrangement plan and still see a debt-review indicator on a credit report. That does not, by itself, prove the bureau has made an error. First establish what the debt counsellor's records show, whether every obligation covered by the arrangement has been satisfied and which formal document or process applies.

Confirm each account's position

Request an up-to-date statement or written confirmation from each relevant credit provider. Check whether any balance, interest, fee or arrears remains and whether all accounts were included in the arrangement. Keep payment-distribution records, bank proof and provider settlement letters. A payment record alone may not be the same as a final account settlement confirmation.

Ask the registered debt counsellor for written guidance

Ask whether all obligations under the rearrangement have been met, whether any amount remains outstanding, what court order or consent arrangement applies and what formal process is appropriate. Request a clear list of the documents needed and which party is responsible for issuing or submitting them.

Do not confuse Form 17.2 and Form 19

Debt-review forms have different purposes. Form 17.2 is associated with the assessment and notification stage. Form 19 is a clearance certificate governed by the National Credit Act and regulations. It is not a document a consumer can simply create or purchase. Ask the registered debt counsellor about the requirements applicable to the facts of the case.

If the report does not update

Once you have the relevant formal confirmation, obtain a fresh credit report and compare the exact status. If you believe the bureau information is inaccurate, challenge the specific entry with the bureau and relevant data provider, attaching the documents that support the correction. Section 72 concerns challenges to accuracy; it is not a way to bypass an unresolved debt-review process.

Documents to organise

  • Debt-rearrangement court order or consent arrangement, where applicable.
  • Current statements from the relevant credit providers.
  • Proof of final payments and written settlement confirmations.
  • Written status confirmation from the registered debt counsellor.
  • Any clearance certificate properly issued for the case.
  • A current credit report showing the precise indicator.
Warning: Be cautious of anyone promising instant or guaranteed debt-review removal for a fee. The applicable legal steps and documents matter; a payment receipt alone does not establish that every requirement has been met.
