Evidence checklist for a credit bureau dispute

A useful dispute file is not the biggest file. It is a clear explanation of one specific issue, supported by documents that allow the bureau or data provider to investigate it.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · General educational information

Write the issue in one sentence

Name the account, report date, exact field and factual error. For example: “The report dated [date] shows an unpaid balance of [amount] on the account ending [digits], but the provider's settlement letter dated [date] confirms settlement.” Only use this wording if the letter really proves settlement. A general request to “clear my name” does not identify what must be investigated.

Match evidence to the problem

IssuePotentially relevant evidenceWhat it may establish
Incorrect balanceProvider statement, transaction history, settlement confirmationRecorded balance and effective date
Payment not reflectedBank statement, receipt, allocation confirmationDate, amount and recipient of payment
Wrong identity/accountRelevant identity records and provider correspondenceWhether the account belongs to you
Duplicate listingReports and account references for both entriesWhether entries refer to the same obligation
Incorrect account statusCurrent statement and written provider confirmationStatus confirmed by the data source
Judgment entryCourt order, case details and later court ordersWhat the court actually ordered

Submit a clear dispute

  1. Obtain and save the report.
  2. Identify the provider and account reference.
  3. State what is wrong and what correction or investigation you request.
  4. Attach only relevant supporting documents.
  5. Use the bureau's current official dispute channel.
  6. Ask for written acknowledgement and a reference number.

Understand the 20-business-day period

Section 72(3) of the National Credit Act requires reasonable steps to seek evidence supporting challenged information. Regulation 20(2) specifies that the steps in section 72(3) must be taken within 20 business days after the challenge is filed. The law does not promise that every entry will be deleted in 20 business days. If credible evidence supports the information, it may remain. If the responsible party cannot find credible evidence, the Act provides for removal, subject to its provisions.

Keep a dispute log

Record the report date, submission date, channel used, reference number, documents supplied, responses and follow-up actions. Save the original files and copies of every message. If a response contains evidence, compare it with the exact issue you raised rather than relying on a generic status message.

If the dispute is not resolved

The National Credit Regulator publishes guidance for complaints about disputed consumer credit information. Its guidance explains the process and supporting material. Depending on the facts, section 72(4) provides a further route to the NCR within 20 business days after receiving evidence under section 72(3)(a). This is a separate period from the initial investigation period. Read the current NCR guidance and statutory wording before taking that step.

Privacy: Credit reports contain sensitive financial and identity information. Use official secure channels, share only what is necessary and never send passwords, one-time PINs or banking login details.
