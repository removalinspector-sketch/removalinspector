How to Get Your Free Credit Report in South Africa

Learn how to request and review your South African credit report, compare entries and identify information that needs follow-up.

Your credit report is a record of credit-related information held by a credit bureau. Before paying anyone to “fix” your profile, obtain your own report and identify exactly what is recorded. A credit score alone does not show the full reason an application may have been declined.

1. Request your report directly

Start with a registered credit bureau and use its official consumer channel. Under section 72(1)(b) of the National Credit Act, a consumer has a right to inspect their credit-bureau information without charge once in any 12-month period. A bureau may charge its applicable fee for additional inspections, subject to the law.

Use the bureau’s official website or verified contact details. Do not send your identity document, bank details or passwords to an unsolicited WhatsApp number or social-media account.

2. Check more than the score

  • Confirm your personal details and addresses are correct.
  • Check each account name, account number and credit provider.
  • Compare balances and payment status with statements and proof of payment.
  • Look for duplicate accounts, unfamiliar enquiries or accounts you do not recognise.
  • Check whether a debt-review indicator or judgment entry appears and whether its status matches your documents.

3. Save evidence before disputing

Download or securely save the report and note the bureau, report date, account reference and exact field you believe is wrong. Gather relevant statements, settlement letters, payment confirmations, court orders or correspondence. Do not alter documents or dispute information merely because it is negative.

4. Choose the right next step

An accurate entry that has not yet updated after a recent event may need an account-status update. Information that is factually incorrect may be challenged through the bureau’s dispute process. Debt-review completion, judgment rescission and prescription questions have distinct requirements; a generic “remove my name” request is not enough.

Useful distinction: A credit report can contain accurate negative information. The right to challenge accuracy does not mean every negative entry must be deleted on request.
