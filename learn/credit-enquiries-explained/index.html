Credit enquiries: why are they on your report?

An enquiry is a record that an organisation accessed or requested information from a credit bureau. The presence of an enquiry is not automatically evidence of fraud or an error.

Reviewed 19 November 2026 · General information

Why an enquiry may appear

Enquiries may be associated with a credit application, account review, quotation or another permitted purpose. The report may identify the organisation and date, but the detail shown can vary. Check the context before concluding that an enquiry is unauthorised.

Investigate an unfamiliar enquiry

  1. Record the bureau, date and organisation name.
  2. Check whether you applied for credit, requested a quotation or interacted with that organisation around that date.
  3. Contact the organisation using contact details from its official website and ask what transaction or permissible purpose relates to the enquiry.
  4. If you still believe the record is inaccurate or unauthorised, dispute the specific enquiry with the bureau and provide the response or other relevant evidence.

Protect yourself against identity misuse

If you suspect someone has used your identity, contact the relevant provider and bureau through official channels. Secure your email and financial accounts, change compromised passwords and report suspected criminal conduct to the appropriate authorities. Do not post identity documents or account numbers publicly.

What to include in a dispute

Identify the exact enquiry, date and organisation. Explain why you believe it is inaccurate. Include relevant correspondence or evidence, and ask the bureau to investigate the source and basis for the entry. Keep the acknowledgement and reference number.

Important: A bureau enquiry and a credit application are not always the same thing. Ask what the enquiry represents before requesting removal.
